# Delivering with a paradigm shift

## Delivery is what happens after you've already done the real work

You wrote the design document. Refraction shaped it, per persona. This is the last part — actually getting it into someone's hands. It's the part you have the least control over in the old way of working, and the part you get the most control over here.

## Your reader gets to hold onto their own experience

Once your governed intent reaches someone, what happens to it is theirs. What they've read, where they left off, what they've already seen — that stays with them, on their own device, not on a server somewhere keeping a file on them so it can decide what to show them next time.

This isn't a small technical detail. It's the difference between designing an experience and designing a surveillance system that happens to also show your work. You made something for someone. You didn't sign them up to be tracked in order to receive it.

## You already made this decision. This is what carries it out.

Refraction already shaped your intent for this specific reader. Delivery is just the part where that shaped experience actually reaches them — and reaches them as something they control, not something a server hands down to them based on a profile it's built.

**Vrit** is the name for this, when the deliverable is something to be read. The reader is in control of their own experience of it, the same way you're in control of your own intent before anyone else touches it. Neither of you had to give that up to the other.

## Why this matters more than it looks like it should

A lot of "personalized" experiences aren't actually shaped for the person — they're shaped by a profile a company built by watching that person, then handed back to them as if it were a gift. That's not what refraction does, and it's worth being able to tell the difference, because your reader can usually tell too, even when they can't articulate why something feels invasive rather than considerate.

What you made was shaped for your reader because you decided who they were and what they needed — not because a server was quietly logging their behavior to guess. The reader gets an experience that's actually theirs to hold, not a profile's best guess wearing your design.

## What this gives you

**You don't have to build tracking to be thoughtful.** The consideration you already put into your personas is what shapes the experience — not data collection standing in for the judgment you already exercised.

**Your work stays current for them, without you republishing it.** Because the shaping can happen at the moment they encounter it, what they get reflects your intent as it stands now, not a version frozen the day you first published it.

**One thing you made, genuinely theirs to each reader who receives it.** Not a single fixed version everyone gets identically, and not a version quietly personalized by a system watching them instead of by you.
