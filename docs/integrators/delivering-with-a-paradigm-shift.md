# Delivering with a paradigm shift

## Delivery requires an answer that respects the boundary your system already has

There's nothing trustworthy to deliver until the answering layer knows what your access-control layer already knows about who's asking.

## The asker is a role you already track, not a new concept

Whoever's asking is a role your system already has an answer for — admin, support rep, guest, customer. Refraction doesn't invent this. It connects to it.

## Governance happens where your access control already lives, not in a parallel system nobody reconciles

The old model: the chatbot has its own idea of what's safe to say, separate from and inconsistent with your actual permission system. Two systems, two sources of truth, drifting apart the moment either one changes.

This works the other way: the answering layer checks the same boundary your product already enforces, at the moment it answers — one source of truth, not two that can quietly disagree.

## Why this is a paradigm shift, not a feature

A smarter model with better guardrails prompted into it is still a second, parallel permission system, hand-maintained and eventually inconsistent with the real one. What actually needed to change is that there's only one system deciding what a role can see — and the AI layer finally checks it instead of approximating it.

## What this gives you

**No parallel permission system to maintain.** The answering layer checks the same boundary your product already enforces — one thing to keep correct, not two.

**Consistency without extra work.** A permission change in your existing system is reflected in what the AI will say, immediately, without a separate update to a chatbot-specific ruleset.

**One governed source, every role.** Admin, support rep, and customer all draw from the same knowledge, scoped correctly per role, without three separate configurations to keep in sync.

## What it costs

Checking the real permission boundary at the moment of every answer costs more than letting the model guess at what's probably safe. This is the same tradeoff named in Refraction — a guess is faster; a checked answer is the only kind actually consistent with what your system already promises its users.
