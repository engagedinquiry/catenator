# Delivering with a paradigm shift

## Delivery requires a grounded answer, not just a fluent one

Getting the right answer to the right asker depends on refraction having already happened — there's nothing trustworthy to deliver until the system knows who's asking and what they're owed.

## The asker is a persona, not a session

Whoever's asking is the governed persona this runs on — not a login state, not a chat session ID. An employee and a customer asking the identical question are different personas, regardless of what channel the question came through.

## Governance happens at the moment of answering, not baked into a static FAQ

The old model: a static FAQ, or a model answering the same way regardless of who's asking, with confidence standing in for accuracy. Neither checks anything at the moment it matters — the FAQ because it's frozen, the model because nothing built into it asks who's receiving the answer.

This works the other way: the trust and context conditions are checked at the moment the question is actually asked, against the current source, for the actual persona asking — not assumed once and reused indefinitely.

## Why this is a paradigm shift, not a feature

A better-sounding chatbot isn't a fix. It's the same ungrounded confidence, delivered more smoothly. What actually needed to change is that the system checks something before it answers, instead of only after someone downstream discovers it was wrong.

## What this gives you

**No answer without a check.** If the system can't ground it, it says so — instead of guessing with borrowed confidence.

**Freshness without a rebuild.** Because the check happens at the moment of answering, an update to the source is reflected immediately, not on the next reindex cycle.

**One governed source, every persona.** The employee and the customer both draw from the same underlying knowledge, shaped differently, without maintaining two separate systems.

## What it costs

Checking grounding at the moment of every answer costs more than returning whatever the retrieval step found and hoping. This is the same tradeoff named in Refraction — a pre-approved FAQ is faster; a governed, checked answer costs more per question and is trustworthy in a way the FAQ never was.
