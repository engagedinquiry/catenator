# The mechanics of refraction

You don't build this part by hand. But knowing what it is changes how confidently you rely on everything that came before it.

## Your governing document becomes a record, not a policy nobody reopens

Everything you said — who asks, what they're owed, what needs grounding — gets turned into something structured. Not a permissions doc filed away after launch. A record the system actually checks against, every time it answers.

### Nothing goes in that you didn't already say

If a trust requirement isn't in the record, the system doesn't invent one. Missing means unspecified, not assumed safe.

### One record, several outputs

- **A grounded answer for one persona.** Cited, complete, for whoever's owed that.
- **A simplified, safe answer for another.** Same source, different persona, different shape.
- **A refusal.** When nothing in the record grounds an answer, that's the output — not a confident guess.

## Why this matters, even though you'll never touch it directly

Before this existed, trust was implicit — assumed rather than checked, the same way for every asker. This record is what makes trust a property of the answer, not a hope about the model's mood that day.

### Specification

A specification here is the grounding rule itself, checkable: does this answer trace to a real, current source, for this persona, under these trust conditions. If it doesn't, that's visible before the answer ships, not after someone acts on it.

### Answer as deliverable

Answers aren't generated once and cached forever. They can be regenerated against the current source, for the actual persona asking, each time — not a snapshot from whenever the knowledge base was last indexed.
