# Delivering with a paradigm shift

## Delivery requires a governed guide

Getting the right version of a guide to the right reader depends on refraction having already happened — there's nothing to deliver until your governing document has been shaped, per persona, into something specific. A help center can't hand someone the right guide if nothing about the guide was ever precise enough to be shaped in the first place.

## The reader is a persona, not a ticket category

Whoever's reading — or whatever agent is reading on their behalf — is the governed persona this all runs on. Not a support-tier label, not a customer segment pulled from a CRM. Wherever "reader" appears below, it means the persona in the Refraction sense: an authored decision about who this is and what they need, not an assumption about who's on the other end of the page.

## Governance happens locally, not through a tracked profile

Some help centers already try to do a version of this — a portal that "personalizes" what it shows based on your account history, your past tickets, behavior it's logged about you. That isn't refraction. That's a guess made on your behalf, using data a server collected about you, often without you knowing it was watching.

Catenator's version works the other way. The reader's own context — what they've already read in this guide, what error they actually hit, what they've told the system about themselves — stays with them. It's not built from a tracked profile assembled behind the scenes. The guide shapes itself around what's actually true for this reader, right now, not around a history someone else was quietly compiling.

## Why this is a paradigm shift, not a feature

The old model: a help center holds a profile and decides what to show you based on data it controls, not you. Refraction already argues against this directly — real refraction happens per persona, shaped for whoever's actually receiving it, not inferred from a profile someone else owns.

This matters as much for an agent reading a guide on someone's behalf as it does for a human — an agent troubleshooting an error shouldn't need to hand a support portal its whole task history just to get the right level of explanation back.

The governed guide arrives as raw material, shaped for this reader specifically, without needing a tracked history to justify why. Client-side, in-the-moment shaping is what makes this a real delivery rather than an aspiration every "personalized help center" has claimed and never quite meant.

## What this gives you, once delivery works this way

**No tracking required.** The guide shapes itself from what the reader actually tells it or actually needs in the moment — not from a profile built by watching them.

**Freshness without a rewrite.** Because refraction can happen at the moment someone opens the guide, what they see reflects the governing document as it stands today — not a cached version from whenever the guide was last generated.

**One governing document, every reader.** The beginner and the expert both read from the same underlying understanding of the use-case, shaped differently for each, without you maintaining two guides or a personalization engine to fake the difference.

## What it costs

Shaping a guide at the moment someone reads it means paying that work each time, rather than once, in advance. This is the same tradeoff named in Refraction — a guide authored and approved ahead of time is faster to serve; one shaped on the spot stays current at the cost of doing the work again, every time someone opens it.
