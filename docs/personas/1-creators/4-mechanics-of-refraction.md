# The mechanics of refraction

You don't write this part. But knowing what it is — and what it can produce — changes how confidently you use everything that came before it.

## Your design document becomes a record, not a note

Everything you said — who this is for, what they need, how it should look, what needs remembering — gets turned into something structured. Not by you. Catenator reads what you wrote and builds a record out of it: a version of your intent that can actually be acted on, by a person or a system, without them having to guess.

Think of it the way a good contract works. You didn't write it in legal language yourself — a lawyer took what you agreed to and made it precise, enforceable, unambiguous. The agreement was always yours. The precision is what someone else's expertise adds on top of it. This is the same trade: your intent stays yours, the record just makes it something nothing can quietly walk back from.

### Nothing goes in that you didn't already say

This record isn't invented. It's extracted — pulled directly from your design document, your notes, your sketch, whatever you actually gave it. If something's missing, it's because you haven't said it yet, not because the system assumed something on your behalf. If it ever proposes something you didn't say, that gets flagged as a guess, not presented as your decision.

### One record, several outputs

Once this exists, it can become different things, without you writing any of them separately:

- **A specification.** What a developer or an AI system builds from — precise enough that they're not interpreting your intent, they're executing it.
- **A document.** What a stakeholder reads to understand the decision, in their own language, without you writing a second version just for them.
- **An experience.** What the actual person — the persona you named — receives, shaped the way refraction already described.

You made one thing. It doesn't have to be re-explained three times to reach three different people.

## Why this matters, even though you'll never touch it directly

Before this existed, your intent lived in exactly one place: your head, or a document nobody reread. Everyone downstream — a developer, a project manager, an AI system — worked from their own memory of what you'd said, or their own guess at what you meant. Every one of those was a chance for your actual intent to quietly become something else.

This record is what stops that. Not because you're now required to be more precise than you already were — you're not. Because what you already said finally gets to stay said.

### Specification

A specification is your record, turned into something a developer or an AI system can build from directly — every persona, every dimension you named, every constraint you were explicit about, carried through intact. Nothing in it is a paraphrase of what you said.

> The specification describes what you said, structured so it can be executed rather than interpreted. This is what a developer opens instead of your design document.

Every design has a specification. Every variant too. What was called daunting and difficult is not so today.

They're not translating your intent into their own understanding of it first — the translation already happened, correctly, once, and what they're holding is precise enough that two different developers reading it would build the same thing.

You do not need to speak engineering and they do not need to speak design.

### Experience as deliverable

This isn't a one-way handoff into something you can't see again. What gets generated can be regenerated — as many times as you need, each time reflecting whatever's currently in your record, not a snapshot from whenever it first ran.

That means you're not handing your design off to a black box and hoping it comes back right. You stay in the loop the whole way through: change something in your design document, and the experience it produces changes with it, the same variant regenerated rather than a new one built from scratch.

You get to take it all the way to delivery — the actual thing a persona receives — not just to the point where someone else takes over and you lose visibility into what happens next.
